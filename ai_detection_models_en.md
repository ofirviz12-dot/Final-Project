# AI-Generated Image and Video Detection: Model and Tool Survey

**Graduation Project — Working Document | November 18, 2026**

> This document summarizes **all models, frameworks, and services mentioned in our previous discussion**. It is not an exhaustive catalog of every model available. Checkpoint availability, licensing, performance claims, and pricing may change and must be verified before final selection. Complexity and hardware notes are estimates, not measured local benchmarks.

## 1. Task definitions

| Task | Detection target | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **AI-generated images** | Images generated entirely or mostly by a generative model | Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, GPT Image |
| **Face manipulation / Deepfake** | Synthetic changes to a real person's face or appearance in recorded content | Face swap, facial reenactment |
| **AI-generated videos** | Videos generated by AI, even without human faces | Fully synthetic scenes or actions |

**Important distinction:** A model trained for one task may not work on another. Detecting an AI signal does not establish whether the depicted event happened, and a genuine-looking image does not establish that a caption is truthful.

## 2. Ready-to-run image detectors — relatively accessible starting points

| Model | Approach | Potential project use | Limitations / hardware |
|---|---|---|---|

Example link: [dima806 on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/dima806/ai_vs_real_image_detection).

## 5. Video detection approaches

### 5.1 Image detectors applied to video frames

Extract representative frames from a video, run image detectors, and aggregate their scores. This is **comparatively easy to implement**, and offers a meaningful project contribution through custom frame selection and score aggregation. However, it **does not directly model motion, lip synchronization, or temporal consistency**.

### 5.2 Video-specific detectors

| Model | Core idea | Focus / remarks |
|---|---|---|

## 8. Recommended shortlist for the graduation project

**Stage 1 — images**

1. **UnivFD:** CLIP-based reference detector.
2. **Community Forensics:** Second detector trained on a wide variety of generators.
3. **NPR:** Potentially complementary low-level forensic signal.
4. **dima806:** Simple end-to-end system baseline; not necessarily a final ensemble member.

**Stage 2 — facial deepfakes:** Add **SBI or GenD** as a separate task-specific detector.

**Stage 3 — videos:** Start with frame sampling and image detectors, then consider **FTCN** as a temporal extension.

**Original algorithm direction:** Calibrate scores on validation data, compare simple averaging with fixed-weight and adaptive fusion, and provide an `Inconclusive` outcome for uncertain cases. For videos, investigate efficient frame selection and aggregation.

### Evaluation and integration cautions

- Track **Precision, Recall, F1, ROC-AUC, False Positive Rate, Calibration, and Latency**; for videos also measure frames processed and total runtime.
- Split train/validation/test by **source, generator, or source video**, where applicable, to prevent leakage.
- Test compression, resizing, screenshots, and generators not used for calibration.
- **Do not blindly average** scores for `AI-generated image`, `face manipulation`, and `synthetic video`: the underlying classification tasks differ.
- Verify licenses, pretrained weights, working inference paths, GPU requirements, and dataset terms **before committing** to specific candidates.

## 9. Conclusion

The most practical research core is **UnivFD + Community Forensics + NPR**, followed by **SBI/GenD** for face manipulation and frame-based video analysis. More demanding detectors and commercial APIs can be added as comparisons if time and budget permit. The main objective is to test whether fusion **reduces false positives and improves generalization** versus individual detectors—not merely to combine more models.
